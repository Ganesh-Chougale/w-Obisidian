## 16.2 Central Limit Theorem
`Central Limit Theorem (CLT)`: When we take larger and larger random samples from a population, the distribution of their sample means tends to become approximately normal (bell-shaped) — even if the original population is not normal.  

- example: Suppose people's heights in a population are not normally distributed.  
  * Take many samples of `30` people.  
  * Calculate the `Mean` height of each sample.  
  * The collection of those means will tend to form a bell-shaped distribution.  

`Population` → `Many Samples` → `Sample Means` → `Approximately Normal Distribution`  

### Remember
CLT is about the distribution of `sample means`, not the original data.  
`Larger Sample Size` → `Sample Mean Distribution` → `More Nearly Normal`  

Exam keyword: As sample size increases → sampling distribution of the mean approaches normality.
