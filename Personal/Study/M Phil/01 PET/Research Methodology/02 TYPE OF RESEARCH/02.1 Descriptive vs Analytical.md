# 2.1 Descriptive vs Analytical Research

## Core Difference

The simplest distinction:

> **Descriptive research → What is happening?**
> **Analytical research → Why / what does the evidence tell us?**

---

## 1. Descriptive Research

### Meaning

**Descriptive research describes the characteristics, facts, conditions, or behaviour of a phenomenon as it exists.**

It primarily answers:

> **What is?**

It does not necessarily investigate why something happens.

### Example

Suppose we study Gen-AI users.

A descriptive study might determine:

* How much context users typically provide.
* What types of context they provide.
* How frequently they use Gen-AI.
* What quality of responses they receive.

Example finding:

> "Most users provide task-specific context, while fewer provide information about their desired output format."

This **describes the situation**.

### Common Methods

* Surveys
* Observation
* Case studies
* Questionnaires
* Fact-finding studies

---

# 2. Analytical Research

### Meaning

**Analytical research uses available facts, information, or data and critically analyses them to understand relationships, causes, patterns, or implications.**

It primarily asks:

> **What does the evidence tell us? Why or how might this occur?**

### Example

Using our research topic:

> **The effect of contextual information on the quality of GEN-AI responses.**

An analytical study might compare responses produced under different contextual conditions and analyse whether contextual information is associated with differences in response quality.

For example:

```text
Little context
      ↓
Response quality = X

Relevant context
      ↓
Response quality = Y

Compare X and Y
      ↓
Analyse the relationship
```

The focus is no longer merely **describing the responses**. It is **analysing the evidence to understand the relationship between context and response quality**.

---

# Key Difference

| Descriptive                                      | Analytical                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Describes what exists                            | Analyses existing information/evidence                   |
| Mainly asks **"What?"**                          | Mainly asks **"Why / what does it mean?"**               |
| Describes characteristics                        | Examines relationships, patterns, causes or implications |
| Often fact-finding                               | Goes beyond description                                  |
| Example: What types of context do users provide? | Example: How does context relate to response quality?    |

## Important

The distinction is about the **purpose of the research**, not simply the data collection method.

A study can collect data through a survey and still be analytical if the collected data are subsequently analysed to examine relationships.

---

## Running Example

### Descriptive

> **"What kinds of contextual information do Gen-AI users provide?"**

→ Describes the existing situation.

### Analytical

> **"How does the type/amount of contextual information affect the quality of Gen-AI responses?"**

→ Analyses the relationship between variables.

---
