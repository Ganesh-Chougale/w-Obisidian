## 4.1 Research Methods vs Methodology
`Research Methods`: the actual techniques used to collect or analyze information.  
- example: using surveys, interviews, observations, or experiments to collect data.  
`Research Methodology`: the reasoning behind choosing those methods and how they fit the research problem.  
- example: choosing interviews instead of a survey because the research needs detailed information about people's experiences.  

`Methods` → What tools/techniques are used?
`Methodology` → Method + why logic & psychology behind it


## 4.2 Significance of Research

`Significance of Research`: explains why the research is useful or important and what it can contribute to knowledge or real-world practice.  
- example: research finds that students remember lessons better with short revision sessions. This can help teachers improve how they teach and students improve how they study.  
`Significance` → Why is this research useful in real world?  

## 4.3 Research Process — Overview
`Research Process`: the series of steps followed to carry out research, from identifying a problem to reaching a conclusion.
- example: identifying why students perform poorly → collecting data → analyzing it → finding the reasons → drawing a conclusion.
`Research Process` → Problem → Data → Analysis → Conclusion
