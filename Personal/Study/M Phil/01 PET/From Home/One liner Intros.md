## 1. UNDERSTANDING RESEARCH

* **1.1 Meaning of Research** — A systematic process of finding answers or new knowledge.
* **1.2 Objectives of Research** — The purposes research tries to achieve.
* **1.3 Motivation in Research** — The reasons that drive a researcher to conduct research.

## 2. TYPE OF RESEARCH

* **2.1 Descriptive vs Analytical** — Descriptive tells what exists; analytical explains or examines why/how it exists.
* **2.2 Applied vs Fundamental** — Applied solves practical problems; fundamental develops general knowledge.
* **2.3 Quantitative vs Qualitative** — Quantitative uses numerical data; qualitative studies meanings, experiences, and qualities.
* **2.4 Conceptual vs Empirical** — Conceptual develops ideas or theories; empirical relies on observed or measured evidence.
* **2.5 Other Types** — Other classifications based on purpose, setting, method, or nature of research.

## 3. RESEARCH APPROACHES

* **3.1 Quantitative** — An approach that studies phenomena using measurable numerical data.
* **3.2 Qualitative** — An approach that studies meanings, experiences, and characteristics using non-numerical data.

## 4. UNDERSTANDING RESEARCH

* **4.1 Research Methods vs Methodology** — Methods are the techniques used; methodology is the reasoning behind choosing and using them.
* **4.2 Research and Scientific Method** — Research applies systematic and evidence-based methods to investigate questions.
* **4.3 Significance of Research** — The value or contribution that research provides to knowledge or practice.
* **4.4 Research Process — Overview** — The sequence of steps followed to conduct research from problem to conclusion.

## 5. DEFINING THE RESEARCH PROBLEM

* **5.1 What is a Research Problem?** — A specific question, difficulty, or knowledge gap that requires investigation.
* **5.2 Selecting the Problem** — Choosing a research problem that is relevant, feasible, and worthwhile.
* **5.3 Necessity of Defining the Problem** — A clearly defined problem gives research a focused direction.
* **5.4 Technique of Defining the Problem** — The process of precisely stating what will be studied and within what boundaries.

## 6. LITERATURE SURVEY

* **6.1 Conceptual Literature** — Existing theories, concepts, principles, and ideas related to the topic.
* **6.2 Empirical Literature** — Existing knowledge based on observations, experiments, or collected data.
* **6.3 Previous Research** — Earlier studies conducted on the same or related problem.

## 7. DEVELOPING HYPOTHESES

* **7.1 Working Hypothesis** — A tentative explanation or prediction used to guide an investigation.
* **7.2 Sources of Hypotheses** — The places from which research hypotheses are derived.
* **7.3 Formulating Testable Hypotheses** — Converting an idea into a statement that can be tested with evidence.

## 8. RESEARCH DESIGN

* **8.1 Meaning & Need** — Research design is the overall plan for conducting a study and obtaining reliable answers.
* **8.2 Features of Good Design** — Characteristics that make a research plan efficient, reliable, and suitable for its purpose.
* **8.3 Different Research Designs** — Different plans used depending on the research question and objectives.
* **8.4 Experimental Designs** — Designs where variables are deliberately manipulated to study their effects.
* **8.5 Developing a Research Plan** — Turning the research design into a practical sequence of activities.

## 9. SAMPLING DESIGN

* **9.1 Census vs Sample Survey** — Census studies the entire population; sample survey studies a selected part of it.
* **9.2 Steps in Sampling** — The process of defining, selecting, and studying a sample.
* **9.3 Probability Sampling** — Sampling where population units have a known chance of selection.
* **9.3.1 Simple Random** — Every population unit has an equal chance of selection.
* **9.3.2 Systematic** — Selecting units at regular intervals from an ordered population.
* **9.3.3 Stratified** — Dividing the population into groups and sampling from each group.
* **9.3.4 Cluster / Area** — Dividing the population into clusters and selecting whole or sampled clusters.
* **9.4 Non-Probability Sampling** — Sampling where selection probabilities are unknown.
* **9.4.1 Convenience** — Selecting units that are easiest to access.
* **9.4.1 Judgement** — Selecting units based on the researcher's judgement.
* **9.4.1 Quota** — Selecting units until predetermined group quotas are filled.

## 10. MEASUREMENT

* **10.1 Measurement in Research** — Assigning numbers or symbols to characteristics according to defined rules.
* **10.2 Measurement Scales** — Systems that determine what kind of information measured values represent.
* **10.3 Sources of Error** — Factors that cause measured values to differ from the true values.
* **10.4 Tests of Sound Measurement** — Tests that determine whether a measurement is reliable and valid.

## 11. SCALING

* **11.1 Meaning of Scaling** — Creating a system for representing the intensity or degree of a characteristic.
* **11.2 Scale Classification** — Categorising scales according to their measurement properties.
* **11.3 Scaling Techniques** — Methods used to construct or apply measurement scales.
* **11.4 Scale Construction** — The process of creating a usable and reliable scale.

## 12. DATA COLLECTION

* **12.1 Primary Data** — Original data collected directly by the researcher for the study.
* **12.1.1 Observation** — Collecting data by systematically watching and recording behaviour or events.
* **12.1.2 Interview** — Collecting data by asking questions directly to respondents.
* **12.1.3 Questionnaire** — Collecting data through a structured set of questions answered by respondents.
* **12.1.4 Schedule** — A structured set of questions filled in by an investigator/enumerator based on respondents' answers.
* **12.2 Secondary Data** — Data previously collected by someone else for another or related purpose.
* **12.3 Selecting Appropriate Method** — Choosing the data-collection method that best fits the research requirements.
* **12.4 Case Study Method** — An in-depth study of a particular case, person, group, organisation, or situation.

## 13. PROCESSING OF DATA

* **13.1 Editing** — Checking collected data for errors, omissions, and inconsistencies.
* **13.2 Coding** — Assigning symbols or numbers to data categories for analysis.
* **13.3 Classification** — Grouping data into meaningful categories.
* **13.4 Tabulation** — Arranging data systematically in tables.

## 14. DESCRIPTIVE STATISTICS

* **14.1 Central Tendency** — Measures that represent the typical or central value of data.
* **14.2 Dispersion** — Measures that show how widely data values are spread.
* **14.3 Skewness** — Measures the asymmetry of a distribution.
* **14.4 Measures of Relationship** — Measures describing the relationship between variables.

## 15. CORRELATION & REGRESSION

* **15.1 Simple Regression** — Estimates the relationship between one dependent and one independent variable.
* **15.2 Multiple Correlation** — Measures the combined relationship of several independent variables with one dependent variable.
* **15.3 Multiple Regression** — Predicts one dependent variable using multiple independent variables.
* **15.4 Partial Correlation** — Measures the relationship between two variables while controlling other variables.

## 16. SAMPLING FUNDAMENTALS

* **16.1 Sampling Distributions** — Distributions of a statistic obtained from repeated samples.
* **16.2 Central Limit Theorem** — Under broad conditions, the sampling distribution of the mean approaches normality as sample size increases.
* **16.3 Standard Error** — Measures the variability of a sample statistic across repeated samples.
* **16.4 Estimation** — Using sample information to estimate an unknown population parameter.

## 17. HYPOTHESIS TESTING

* **17.1 Meaning of Hypothesis** — A testable statement about a population or relationship between variables.
* **17.2 Basic Concepts** — The fundamental ideas needed to understand statistical hypothesis testing.
* **17.3 Procedure** — The systematic steps used to test a hypothesis statistically.
* **17.4 Power of a Test** — The probability that a test correctly detects a real effect.

## 18. PARAMETRIC TESTS

* **18.1 Tests of Means** — Tests used to determine whether population means differ or match a specified value.
* **18.1 Difference between Means** — Tests whether two population means differ significantly.
* **18.1 Proportions** — Tests whether population proportions differ or match a specified value.
* **18.1 Variances** — Tests concerning population variance or differences between variances.
* **18.1 Correlation Coefficients** — Tests whether a population correlation is statistically significant.

## 19. CHI-SQUARE TEST

* **Chi-Square Test** — A statistical test commonly used for categorical data to test association or goodness of fit.

## 20. ANOVA / ANOCOVA

* **ANOVA / ANOCOVA** — ANOVA compares means across groups; ANOCOVA compares means while controlling another variable.

## 21. NON-PARAMETRIC TESTS

* **Non-Parametric Tests** — Statistical tests that generally do not require strong assumptions about population distributions.

## 22. MULTIVARIATE ANALYSIS

* **22.1 Characteristics & Applications** — Multivariate analysis studies several variables simultaneously to understand complex relationships.
* **22.2 Classification** — Grouping multivariate techniques according to their purpose and mathematical structure.
* **22.3 Variables** — The measurable characteristics included in multivariate analysis.
* **22.4 Factor Analysis** — Reducing many observed variables into a smaller number of underlying factors.
* **22.5 Factor Rotation** — Transforming factors to make their interpretation clearer.
* **22.6 R-Type & Q-Type Analysis** — R-type studies relationships among variables; Q-type studies similarities among cases or respondents.
* **22.7 Path Analysis** — Examining hypothesised direct and indirect relationships among variables.

## 23. INTERPRETATION

* **23.1 Meaning of Interpretation** — Giving meaningful explanations to the results obtained from research.
* **23.2 Why Interpretation?** — To connect statistical results with the research problem and draw meaningful conclusions.
* **23.3 Technique of Interpretation** — Methods used to logically explain and evaluate research findings.
* **23.4 Precautions** — Steps taken to avoid incorrect, exaggerated, or unsupported conclusions.

## 24. RESEARCH REPORT WRITING

* **24.1 Steps in Writing** — The sequence followed to organise and write the research findings.
* **24.2 Layout of Research Report** — The standard structure and arrangement of a research report.
* **24.3 Types of Reports** — Different forms of research reports designed for different audiences and purposes.
* **24.4 Oral Presentation** — Communicating research findings verbally to an audience.
* **24.5 Mechanics of Writing** — The rules and practical details governing clear and proper research writing.

## 25. PRECAUTIONS

* **Precautions** — Measures taken to minimise errors, bias, and incorrect conclusions throughout research.

## 26. COMPUTER IN RESEARCH

* **26.1 Computer System** — The hardware and software components used to process research information.
* **26.2 Computer Technology** — Technologies that support storing, processing, analysing, and communicating research data.
* **26.3 Computer Applications** — Ways computers are used in different stages of research.
* **26.4 Computers and Researcher** — How computers assist researchers in performing research tasks efficiently.
