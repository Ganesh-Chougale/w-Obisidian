## 14.2 Dispersion
`Dispersion`: Measures that show **how spread out or scattered** the data values are around the centre.  

- example:
  `A = 48, 49, 50, 51, 52` → values are **close together** → low dispersion.  
  `B = 10, 30, 50, 70, 90` → values are **widely spread** → high dispersion.  

Common measures of dispersion:  
- `Range` → Difference between the highest and lowest value.  
- `Variance` → Measures how far values spread from the mean.  
- `Standard Deviation` → Shows the typical amount of spread around the mean.  

`Central Tendency` → **Where is the centre?**  
`Dispersion` → **How spread out is the data?**  

**Remember:**  
`Low dispersion` = values are close together.  
`High dispersion` = values are far apart.
