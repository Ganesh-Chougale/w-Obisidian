# Biggest Mistake Beginners Make with **Stock P/E**

### Mistake:

```text
Low P/E = cheap stock = good investment ❌
```

This is **very often wrong**.

---

# Why this mistake happens

People think:

```text
Lower price compared to profit = good deal
```

But they forget **why the market priced it low**.

Markets are not stupid most of the time.

---

# Example

### Company A

| Item        | Value |
| ----------- | ----- |
| Share Price | ₹100  |
| EPS         | ₹20   |

```text
P/E = 5
```

Looks **very cheap**.

But reality might be:

* industry dying
* profits falling
* management problems
* debt issues

Market expects **future profits to drop**.

So investors are not willing to pay more.

---

### Company B

| Item        | Value |
| ----------- | ----- |
| Share Price | ₹1000 |
| EPS         | ₹20   |

```text
P/E = 50
```

Looks **very expensive**.

But reality might be:

* profits growing 25% yearly
* strong brand
* dominant market position
* huge future demand

Investors expect **future profits to grow a lot**.

---

# The key truth

```text
P/E compares price with today's profit
```

But the market cares about:

```text
future profits
```

---

# Simple analogy

Imagine two mango trees.

### Tree A

* gives **10 mangoes today**
* next year maybe **5 mangoes**

Price = cheap.

---

### Tree B

* gives **10 mangoes today**
* next year maybe **30 mangoes**

Price = expensive.

---

Investors care about **future mangoes**, not just today's.

---

# Simple rule experienced investors follow

```text
Low P/E + good business = interesting
Low P/E + bad business = value trap
High P/E + fast growth = sometimes justified
```

---

# One sentence to remember

```text
P/E tells price of today's profit,
not the value of tomorrow's business.
```
