# `ROE (Return on Equity)`

### ROE = `how efficiently a company uses shareholders' money to generate profit`

When you buy shares, you give **money to the company**.
That money becomes **shareholder equity**.

ROE tells:

```text 
How much profit the company generates
from shareholders' money
```

---

# Formula

```text 
ROE = (Net Profit / Shareholder Equity) × 100
```

Where:

| Term               | Meaning                               |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Net Profit         | total profit after all expenses       |
| Shareholder Equity | total money belonging to shareholders |

---

# What is **Shareholder Equity**

Simple meaning:

```text 
Shareholder Equity = Total Assets − Total Liabilities
```

This is basically the **company’s net worth**.

You may notice:

```text
Book Value is derived from this equity
```

(Book value = equity per share)

---

# Simple example

Assume a company has:

| Item              | Value      |
| ----------------- | ---------- |
| Shareholder money | ₹100 crore |
| Net profit        | ₹20 crore  |

Calculation:

```text 
ROE = (20 / 100) × 100
ROE = 20%
```

Meaning:

```text
For every ₹100 of shareholder money,
the company generates ₹20 profit per year
```

---

# Real-life analogy

Imagine two businesses.

### Business A

```text 
Owner investment = ₹10 lakh
Profit = ₹2 lakh
ROE = 20%
```

---

### Business B

```text 
Owner investment = ₹10 lakh
Profit = ₹80,000
ROE = 8%
```

Business A uses **owner money much more efficiently**.

---

# Simple guideline investors use

| ROE    | Meaning |
| ------ | ------- |
| <10%   | weak    |
| 10–15% | average |
| 15–20% | good    |

> `>20%` | excellent |

(rough rule, varies by industry)

---

# Key difference between **ROCE vs ROE**

| Metric | Measures                                           |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| ROCE   | profit generated from **total business capital**   |
| ROE    | profit generated from **shareholders’ money only** |

---

# Example difference

Assume a company uses:

| Source            | Amount     |
| ----------------- | ---------- |
| Shareholder money | ₹100 crore |
| Bank loans        | ₹100 crore |

Total business capital:

```text 
₹200 crore
```

If profit is ₹20 crore:

```text
ROCE = 20 / 200 = 10%
ROE  = 20 / 100 = 20%
```

ROE looks higher because **loans amplify returns**.

---

# Important warning ⚠️

Very high ROE can sometimes happen because of:

```text 
high debt
```

So investors often check **ROE together with ROCE**.
