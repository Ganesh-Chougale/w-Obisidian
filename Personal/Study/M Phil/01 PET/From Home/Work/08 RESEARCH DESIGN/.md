## 8.1 Meaning & Need
`Research Design`: the overall plan or blueprint of a research study. It decides how data will be collected, analyzed, and used to answer the research question.  
- example: if you want to study whether online classes improve student performance, the research design decides who will be studied, what data will be collected, and how the results will be compared.  
`Research Design` → Research Question → Plan → Data → Answer  
Need of Research Design: It gives the research a clear direction and helps avoid wasted time, unreliable data, and unnecessary work.  
---

## 8.2 Features of Good Design
A good research design should make the study clear, practical, reliable, and suitable for the research problem.  
* Clear → every step is properly planned.  
* Suitable → matches the research question and objectives.  
* Reliable → produces consistent and trustworthy results.  
* Valid → actually measures what the research intends to measure.  
* Flexible → allows reasonable changes when required.  
* Economical → makes good use of available time, money, and resources.  
* example: if studying student satisfaction, using a well-designed questionnaire with the right group of students is better than randomly asking a few students.  
`Good Research Design` → Clear + Suitable + Reliable + Valid + Practical  
---

## 8.3 Different Research Designs
`Research Designs`: different ways of planning a study, chosen according to what the researcher wants to find out.  
Common designs include:    
* Exploratory → used when the problem is not clearly understood.  
  * example: interviewing students to explore why they avoid online classes.  
* Descriptive → describes what exists or happens.  
  * example: finding how many students attend online classes regularly.  
* Analytical → examines relationships or reasons behind something.  
  * example: studying whether class attendance is related to exam performance.  
* Experimental → changes one variable to observe its effect on another.  
  * example: giving one group a new teaching method and comparing its results with another group.  
* Historical → studies past events using available records and evidence.  
  * example: studying how India's education system changed after independence.  
`Research Question` → Choose the design that fits the question  
---

## 8.4 Experimental Designs
`Experimental Design`: a research design where the researcher deliberately changes one variable and observes whether it causes a change in another variable.

* example: two groups of students are given different teaching methods:

  * Group A → traditional teaching
  * Group B → AI-assisted teaching
  * compare their exam performance.

Here:
`Teaching Method` → Independent Variable
`Exam Performance` → Dependent Variable

The researcher changes the teaching method and observes its effect on exam performance.  
`Experimental Design` → Change something → Observe the effect → Compare results
---

## 8.5 Developing a Research Plan
`Research Plan`: converting the research design into a step-by-step practical plan for actually conducting the study.  

A research plan usually decides:  
* What → research problem and objectives  
* Who/What → population and sample  
* How → methods of collecting data  
* When → schedule and time required  
* How to analyze → techniques for analyzing the data  
* Resources → people, money, tools, and materials required  
* example: for studying AI-assisted learning among MCA students:
  select students → prepare questionnaire → collect responses → analyze data → interpret findings → write report  

`Research Design` → Overall Blueprint  
`Research Plan` → Practical Steps to Execute the Blueprint
