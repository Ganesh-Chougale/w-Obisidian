# `Stock P/E (Price to Earnings)`

### Stock P/E = `how many rupees investors pay for ₹1 of company profit`

That’s the **entire idea**.

---

## The formula

P/E = (Price Per Share) / (Earnings Per Share)

Meaning:

| Part                     | Meaning                        |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Price Per Share          | market price of 1 share        |
| Earnings Per Share (EPS) | profit company earns per share |

---

# Step-by-step example

Assume a company:

| Item                   | Value |
| ---------------------- | ----- |
| Share price            | ₹200  |
| Profit per share (EPS) | ₹10   |

Calculation:

```
P/E = 200 / 10
P/E = 20
```

---

# What **P/E = 20** actually means

It means:

```
Investors are paying ₹20
to buy ₹1 of yearly profit
```

That’s it.

Not more complicated than that.

---

# Another simple example

| Share Price | EPS | P/E |
| ----------- | --- | --- |
| ₹100        | ₹10 | 10  |
| ₹300        | ₹10 | 30  |

Both companies earn **₹10 profit per share**.

But:

* Company A price = ₹100
* Company B price = ₹300

So investors are **paying more for the same profit**.

That’s why **P/E is higher**.

---

# Shop example  

Imagine a shop.

| Item               | Value    |
| ------------------ | -------- |
| Shop yearly profit | ₹10 lakh |
| Shop selling price | ₹2 crore |

Calculation:

```
P/E = 2,00,00,000 / 10,00,000
P/E = 20
```

Meaning:

```
Buyer is paying 20× yearly profit to buy the business
```

Exactly the same idea in stocks.

---

# Why investors still buy high P/E stocks

Because they expect **profit to grow**.

Example:

| Year | Profit |
| ---- | ------ |
| 2024 | ₹10    |
| 2025 | ₹15    |
| 2026 | ₹25    |

If profits grow fast, today's high P/E may become normal later.

---

# Simple mental shortcut

```
Low P/E  → cheaper profit
High P/E → expensive profit
```

But:

```
Cheap profit sometimes = bad company
Expensive profit sometimes = great company
```

---

# One line to remember forever

```
Stock P/E = price investors pay for ₹1 of company profit
```

---

### Very simple interpretation

| P/E   | Rough Meaning            |
| ----- | ------------------------ |
| 10    | cheap valuation          |
| 15–25 | normal range             |
| 40+   | expensive or high growth |

⚠️ These are **not strict rules**.

---

### Important warning ⚠️

```text
Low P/E ≠ good company
High P/E ≠ bad company
```
