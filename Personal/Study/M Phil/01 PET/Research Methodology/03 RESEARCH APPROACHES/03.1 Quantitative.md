# 3.1 Quantitative Research Approach

## Core Idea

**The quantitative approach uses numerical measurement and statistical analysis to study phenomena, relationships, differences, or effects.**

Mental model:

```text
Research Question
      ↓
Define Variables
      ↓
Measure Variables
      ↓
Collect Numerical Data
      ↓
Statistical Analysis
      ↓
Results / Conclusion
```

---

## Key Characteristics

### 1. Numerical Data

Information is expressed in numbers.

**Our example:**

Response quality could be measured on a scale from **1–10**.

```text
No context       → 4/10
Basic context    → 6/10
Relevant context → 8/10
```

### 2. Measurement

The researcher defines variables that can be measured.

For our research:

* **Independent variable:** contextual information
* **Dependent variable:** quality of Gen-AI response

The researcher can then investigate whether changes in contextual information are associated with changes in response quality.

### 3. Structured Data Collection

Quantitative research generally uses structured methods that produce data suitable for numerical analysis.

Examples:

* Structured questionnaires
* Tests
* Experiments
* Measurements
* Structured observations

### 4. Statistical Analysis

Numerical data can be analysed using statistical techniques.

Examples include:

* Mean
* Percentage
* Correlation
* Regression
* t-test
* ANOVA
* Chi-square

The appropriate technique depends on the research question and type of data.

### 5. Objectivity and Control

Quantitative research generally attempts to make measurement and analysis as **systematic and objective** as possible.

# Our Research Example

Research topic:

> **The effect of contextual information on the quality of GEN-AI responses.**

A quantitative approach could be:

```text
Same AI task
      ↓
Change contextual information
      ↓
Generate responses
      ↓
Assign quality scores
      ↓
Collect numerical results
      ↓
Statistical analysis
      ↓
Determine whether measurable differences/relationships exist
```

For example:

| Context condition         | Average quality score |
| ------------------------- | --------------------: |
| No context                |                   4.2 |
| Basic context             |                   6.5 |
| Relevant detailed context |                   8.1 |

The numbers allow us to quantitatively examine the relationship between context and response quality.

---

## Strengths

Quantitative research is useful when we want to:

* Measure something precisely.
* Compare groups or conditions.
* Identify numerical differences.
* Examine relationships between variables.
* Test hypotheses statistically.
* Summarise patterns using numerical evidence.

---

## Limitations

Numbers do not always explain **why** something happens.

For example:

> A study may show that relevant context increases the average AI response-quality score from 6 to 8.

But the numbers alone may not explain:

> **Why did the context improve the response?**

That deeper question may require a **qualitative approach**.
