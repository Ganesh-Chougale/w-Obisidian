# 5.3 Necessity of Defining the Problem

## Core Idea

A research problem must be **clearly and precisely defined before the actual investigation begins**.

> **An unclear problem → unclear research → wasted effort / unreliable conclusions.**

Defining the problem gives the research a clear direction.

---

## Why Is It Necessary?

### 1. Gives Clear Direction

It tells the researcher **exactly what to investigate** and what not to investigate.

> "Gen-AI" is too broad.

> "Effect of contextual information on Gen-AI response quality" is focused.

---

### 2. Helps Set Clear Objectives

Once the problem is clear, the researcher can determine **what the study should accomplish**.

**Problem → Objectives**

---

### 3. Helps Formulate Hypotheses

A precise problem makes it easier to identify variables and formulate a **testable hypothesis**.

**Problem → Variables → Hypothesis**

---

### 4. Guides Research Design

The problem determines important decisions about **how the research should be conducted**.

For example:

* What data is required?
* Who/what should be studied?
* How should data be collected?
* Which methods should be used?

**Problem → Research Design**

---

### 5. Prevents Unnecessary Data Collection

Without a clearly defined problem, researchers may collect large amounts of irrelevant information.

> **Define first → collect relevant data.**

---

### 6. Makes the Scope Clear

It establishes the **boundaries of the study**.

For example, a Gen-AI study might specify:

* which Gen-AI systems
* what type of tasks
* what kind of contextual information
* how response quality is measured

This prevents the study from becoming unnecessarily broad.

---

### 7. Makes Analysis and Interpretation Easier

When the research question is precise, the researcher knows what relationships, differences, or effects need to be examined.

---

### 8. Saves Time and Resources

Clear definition prevents the researcher from going in irrelevant directions and wasting:

* time
* money
* effort
* data
* other resources

---

## Mental Model

```text
UNDEFINED / BROAD IDEA
          ↓
   DEFINE THE PROBLEM
          ↓
   Clear Question + Scope
          ↓
       Objectives
          ↓
      Hypothesis
          ↓
   Research Design
          ↓
   Relevant Data
          ↓
  Analysis & Interpretation
```

The key idea is:

> **The research problem acts like the steering wheel of the entire research process.**

---

## One-second Memory

> **Define the problem to give research direction, scope, relevant data, suitable methods, and meaningful conclusions.**

### PET Trap

Do not confuse:

**Selecting the problem** → *Which problem should I study?*

**Defining the problem** → *Exactly what does this problem mean, and what are its boundaries?*
