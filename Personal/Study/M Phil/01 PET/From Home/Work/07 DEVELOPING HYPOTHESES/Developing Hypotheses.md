## 7.1 Working Hypothesis
`Working Hypothesis`: a temporary prediction or possible explanation that the researcher uses as a guide and later tests with evidence.  
- example: *Students who use AI for regular revision will score higher in exams than students who do not.*  
`Working Hypothesis` → Make a prediction → Test it → Accept or reject based on evidence

## 7.2 Sources of Hypotheses
`Sources of Hypotheses`: the places or experiences from which a researcher gets ideas for forming a hypothesis.  
- example: a researcher may get a hypothesis from previous research, existing theories, personal observations, or real-world problems.  
`Sources` → Where does the research idea come from?  

## 7.3 Formulating Testable Hypotheses
`Formulating Testable Hypotheses`: turning a general idea into a clear statement that can be checked using data or evidence.
- example:
  General idea: AI may help students study better.
  → Testable hypothesis: *Students who use AI for revision will score higher in exams than students who do not.*
`Testable Hypothesis` → Clear statement → Measurable variables → Can be tested with data.
