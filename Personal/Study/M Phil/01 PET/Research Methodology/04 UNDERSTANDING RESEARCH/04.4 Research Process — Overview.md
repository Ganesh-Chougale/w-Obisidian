# 4.4 Research Process — Overview

## Core Idea

The **research process** is the systematic sequence of steps followed to conduct research.

### The Big Picture

**PROBLEM → LITERATURE → HYPOTHESIS → DESIGN → DATA → ANALYSIS → INTERPRETATION → REPORT**

Think of research as moving from:

> **"I don't know." → "I investigate." → "Now I have evidence."**

---

## Main Steps

### 1. Formulate the Research Problem

Identify and clearly define **what needs to be investigated**.

> Example: Does contextual information affect the quality of Gen-AI responses?

### 2. Review the Literature

Study existing theories, research, findings, and knowledge related to the problem.

Purpose:

* understand what is already known
* identify gaps
* avoid unnecessary duplication
* build a foundation for the study

### 3. Develop a Hypothesis

Formulate a **testable statement or assumption** about the expected relationship between variables, when appropriate.

> Example: "Relevant contextual information improves Gen-AI response quality."

### 4. Prepare the Research Design

Decide **how the research will actually be conducted**.

This includes decisions about:

* what data is needed
* from whom/what
* how data will be collected
* how variables will be measured
* how data will be analysed

### 5. Collect Data

Gather the required evidence using appropriate methods.

Examples:

* observation
* interview
* questionnaire
* experiment
* existing/secondary data

### 6. Analyse the Data

Organise and examine the collected data to find:

* patterns
* relationships
* differences
* effects
* trends

Statistical methods may be used in quantitative research.

### 7. Interpret the Results

Determine **what the findings mean in relation to the research problem**.

> Example: Does the evidence actually support the claim that context improves response quality?

### 8. Write and Present the Report

Communicate:

* the problem
* methodology
* data
* analysis
* findings
* conclusions
* recommendations, where appropriate

---

## Important PET Point

The research process is **systematic**, but it is not always a rigid one-way process.

Research may move backward when necessary.

For example:

**Analysis → discovers a problem → revisit literature → modify interpretation**

So remember:

> **Research is a structured process, not necessarily a perfectly straight line.**

## Kothari-style exam memory

The broad sequence to remember is:

**Formulating the Research Problem → Extensive Literature Survey → Developing Hypotheses → Preparing the Research Design → Determining Sample Design → Collecting Data → Execution → Analysis → Hypothesis Testing → Generalisations and Interpretation → Preparation of the Report**

The detailed Kothari sequence becomes especially important later because chapters such as **problem definition, literature survey, hypotheses, research design, sampling, data collection, processing, analysis, interpretation, and report writing** each expand one part of this process.
