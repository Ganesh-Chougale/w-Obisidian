# `EPS (Earnings Per Share)`

### EPS = `profit earned by the company for each share`

It answers a very simple question:

```text
If the company profit is divided equally,
how much profit belongs to one share?
```

---

# Formula

```text
EPS = Net Profit / Total Shares
```

Where:

| Term         | Meaning                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Net Profit   | total profit company earned    |
| Total Shares | number of shares investors own |

---

# Simple example

Suppose a company earns:

| Item         | Value      |
| ------------ | ---------- |
| Total Profit | ₹100 crore |
| Total Shares | 10 crore   |

Calculation:

```text
EPS = 100 / 10
EPS = ₹10
```

Meaning:

```text
Each share represents ₹10 profit of the company
```

---

# Why EPS matters

Many important metrics come from EPS.

### Example: P/E

You already learned:

```text
P/E = Share Price / EPS
```

Example:

| Share Price | ₹200 |
| ----------- | ---- |
| EPS         | ₹10  |

```text
P/E = 200 / 10 = 20
```

---

# Another example

Two companies:

| Company   | Profit     | Shares   | EPS |
| --------- | ---------- | -------- | --- |
| Company A | ₹100 crore | 10 crore | ₹10 |
| Company B | ₹100 crore | 50 crore | ₹2  |

Both made **same total profit**, but:

```text
Company A gives more profit per share
```

So investors often prefer **higher EPS**.

---

# Simple analogy

Imagine a pizza 🍕

Company profit = whole pizza
Shares = number of slices

```text
EPS = size of each slice
```

More slices → smaller piece per person.

---

# How EPS connects to everything you learned

| Metric          | Uses EPS?     |
| --------------- | ------------- |
| P/E             | YES           |
| ROE             | indirectly    |
| Dividend        | often related |
| Stock valuation | YES           |

EPS is basically **the foundation of stock profitability**.
