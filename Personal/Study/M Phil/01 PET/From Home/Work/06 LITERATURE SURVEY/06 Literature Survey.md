## 6.1 Conceptual Literature
`Conceptual Literature`: existing theories, concepts, principles, and ideas that help explain or understand the research topic.
- example: while researching why people adopt a new technology, studying existing concepts such as Technology Acceptance and the factors that influence people's willingness to use technology.
`Conceptual Literature` → Existing Ideas → Understand the Topic → Build the Research Foundation


## 6.2 Empirical Literature
`Empirical Literature`: existing research based on actual observations, experiments, surveys, or collected data.  
- example: a previous study surveyed 500 smartphone users and found that people who use a phone for more than 6 hours a day reported lower sleep quality.  
`Empirical Literature` → Actual Data → Findings → Evidence  

## 6.3 Previous Research
`Previous Research`: studies that were already conducted on the same or a related research problem.  
- example: before studying *"the effect of AI-assisted study on MCA students"*, reviewing earlier studies that investigated AI use and student academic performance.  
`Previous Research` → What has already been studied? → What did they find? → What is still left to study?
