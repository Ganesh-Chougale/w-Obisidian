# `ROCE (Return on Capital Employed)`
- in simple words `Return on Money Used`  

### ROCE = `how efficiently a company uses its money to generate profit`
❌ if ROCE is `20%` after investing 100₹ i'll earn 20₹ from it in return  
✅️ The business generates ₹20 operating profit for every ₹100 used in the business.  

A company needs **money (capital)** to run:

* factories
* machines
* offices
* inventory
* working capital

ROCE tells:

```text
How much profit the company generates
from the money invested in the business
```

---

# Formula

```text
ROCE = (Operating Profit / Capital Employed) × 100
```

Where:

| Term             | Meaning                          |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Operating Profit | profit from business operations  |
| Capital Employed | total money used in the business |

---

# What is **Capital Employed**

Simple meaning:

```text
Capital Employed =
Total Assets − Current Liabilities
```

Or you can think of it as:

```text
Total money invested in the business
```

Sources of that money can be:

* shareholder money  
* company reserves  
* long-term loans  

---

# Simple example

Assume a company has:

| Item                   | Value      |
| ---------------------- | ---------- |
| Money used in business | ₹100 crore |
| Operating profit       | ₹20 crore  |

Calculation:

```text
ROCE = (20 / 100) × 100
ROCE = 20%
```

Meaning:

```text
For every ₹100 used in the business,
the company generates ₹20 profit per year.
```

---

# Why ROCE is powerful

It shows **business efficiency**.

Example:

| Company   | Capital    | Profit    | ROCE |
| --------- | ---------- | --------- | ---- |
| Company A | ₹100 crore | ₹20 crore | 20%  |
| Company B | ₹100 crore | ₹10 crore | 10%  |

Company A uses money **more efficiently**.

Investors usually prefer **higher ROCE**.

---

# Real-world analogy

Imagine two shops.

### Shop A

```text
Investment = ₹10 lakh
Profit = ₹2 lakh
ROCE = 20%
```

---

### Shop B

```text
Investment = ₹10 lakh
Profit = ₹80,000
ROCE = 8%
```

Both invested the same money.

But **Shop A uses money much better**.

---

# Simple rule many investors follow

| ROCE   | Meaning       |
| ------ | ------------- |
| <10%   | weak business |
| 10–15% | average       |
| 15–20% | good          |

> `>20%` | excellent |

(rough guideline, not a strict rule)

---

# Important insight

Companies with **consistently high ROCE** often become:

* dominant businesses
* wealth creators
* long-term compounders

Because they **turn money into profit efficiently**.
