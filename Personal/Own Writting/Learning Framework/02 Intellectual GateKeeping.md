# Goal: Getting rid of intellectual gatekeeping  

When we want to learn about a new topic, we choose our learning materials—like books, research papers, or articles. However, learners often face two major barriers:
1. `The Use of Jargon`: Experts use specialized terms to explain complex ideas quickly. While this makes technical writing shorter, it can be confusing for beginners. Over time, we usually get used to these terms.
2. `Intellectual Gatekeeping`: This happens when authors and experts intentionally wrap simple, universal ideas in overly complicated, fancy language. They do this to make themselves look important, unique, or highly academic.

This gatekeeping creates unnecessary barriers. It makes learning feel much harder than it actually is, leading to frustration when you try to master something new.

## Examples of the gatekeeping sentaces
Intellectual gatekeeping often uses passive voice, overly long sentence structures, and abstract framing to state incredibly simple concepts.
### example 1:
* `Gatekeeping`: "The structural framework of the contemporary workplace environment necessitates a robust paradigm of bidirectional interpersonal information exchange to optimize operational efficacy."
* `Simpler version`: "Good workplace communication helps employees do their jobs effectively."
### example 2:
* `Gatekeeping`: "An examination of historical precedents indicates that individual agents frequently manifest behavioral adaptations aligned with minimizing cognitive dissonance when confronted with destabilizing socio-economic phenomena."
* `Simpler version`: "History shows that people change how they act to feel better when times get tough."
### example 3:
* `Gatekeeping`: "The implementation of the newly synthesized pedagogical methodology yielded a statistically significant enhancement in the retention of fundamental conceptual schemas among the cohort."
* `Simpler version`: "The new teaching method helped students remember their lessons better."
### example 4:
* `Gatekeeping`: "We must interrogate the intersectional modalities of systemic infrastructural inequalities to destabilize entrenched socioeconomic hierarchies."
* `Simpler version`: "We need to look closely at how broken systems keep people poor so we can fix them."

## Examples of the gatekeeping words
Unlike valid technical jargon used for scientific precision (e.g., "neuroplasticity" or "photosynthesis"), these words are often substituted for ordinary verbs and nouns simply to add an artificial layer of academic prestige.

| Gatekeeping Word | Simpler version Equivalent |
|---|---|
| Bifurcate | Split / Divide into two |
| Praxis | Practice / Action |
| Modalities | Ways / Methods |
| Efficacy | Effectiveness |
| Juxtaposition | Comparison / Contrast |
| Obfuscate | Hide / Confuse / Make unclear |
