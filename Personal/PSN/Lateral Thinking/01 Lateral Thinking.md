Lateral thinking is a method of solving problems through an indirect and creative approach. Instead of following a predictable, step-by-step logical sequence, it involves looking at a problem from a completely new angle and challenging underlying assumptions.

The term was coined by psychologist Edward de Bono in 1967. He famously described the difference like this: logic (vertical thinking) is used to dig a hole deeper and bigger, while lateral thinking is used to dig a hole in an entirely different place.

## Lateral vs. Vertical Thinking

To understand lateral thinking, it helps to compare it to the traditional analytical thinking we are usually taught in school:

|  | Vertical Thinking (Logical) | Lateral Thinking (Creative) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Approach** | Sequential and step-by-step. | Jumps around; explores alternative paths. |
| **Goal** | To find the single "right" answer. | To generate as many alternative approaches as possible. |
| **Process** | Evaluates and selects the most promising idea. | Welcomes intrusions, random elements, and "wrong" ideas to spark new connections. |
| **Handling Assumptions** | Takes standard constraints for granted. | Actively challenges rules and questions "obvious" boundaries. |

## A Classic Example

Lateral thinking often involves realizing that your brain has added constraints to a problem that aren't actually there.

> **The Scenario:** A man lives on the 10th floor of a building. Every morning, he takes the elevator all the way down to the ground floor and goes to work. When he returns from work, he takes the elevator to the 7th floor, gets out, and walks up the stairs the rest of the way to his apartment. Why?

If you use purely vertical thinking, you might assume the elevator is broken above the 7th floor, or he wants exercise (but why only on the way up?).

**The Lateral Answer:** The man is very short. He can reach the button for the ground floor, but he can only reach as high as the 7th-floor button.

The riddle works because we unconsciously assume that any adult riding an elevator can reach all the buttons. Lateral thinking forces you to spot that unstated assumption and discard it.
