## 14.4 Measures of Relationship
`Measures of Relationship`: Statistical measures that tell us how two or more variables are related to each other.

In simple words:
> “When one variable changes, what happens to the other?”

- example: Study time ↔ Exam marks
  * More study time → generally higher marks → positive relationship
- example: Price ↔ Demand
  * Price increases → demand generally decreases → negative relationship
- example: Shoe size ↔ Exam marks
  * No meaningful connection → little/no relationship

A common measure is Correlation, which tells us the direction and strength of the relationship.

Remember:
`Central Tendency` → centre
`Dispersion` → spread
`Skewness` → asymmetry
`Relationship` → connection between variables
