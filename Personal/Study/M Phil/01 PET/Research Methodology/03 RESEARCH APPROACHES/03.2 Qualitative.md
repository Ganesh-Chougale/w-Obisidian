# 3.2 Qualitative Research Approach

## Core Idea

**The qualitative approach studies meanings, experiences, perceptions, behaviours, and processes using non-numerical information.**

Mental model:

```text
Research Question
      ↓
Explore Experiences / Behaviour
      ↓
Collect Words / Observations
      ↓
Identify Patterns & Themes
      ↓
Interpret
      ↓
Understanding
```

---

## Key Characteristics

### 1. Non-Numerical Data

The primary information is usually:

* Words
* Descriptions
* Interviews
* Observations
* Documents
* Experiences
* Opinions

The focus is on **meaning and understanding**, rather than primarily on numerical measurement.

---

### 2. Open-Ended Investigation

Qualitative research often allows participants to explain things in their own words.

Instead of:

> "Rate the usefulness of context from 1–10."

we might ask:

> **"What kind of context helps Gen-AI give you a better response, and why?"**

This can reveal information that a numerical score alone cannot capture.

---

### 3. Researcher Seeks Meaning

The researcher looks for:

* Patterns
* Themes
* Explanations
* Experiences
* Perceptions
* Relationships within the participants' accounts

---

## Common Methods

Important methods include:

* **Interviews**
* **Observation**
* **Focus groups**
* **Case studies**
* **Document analysis**

---

# Our Research Example

Research topic:

> **The effect of contextual information on the quality of GEN-AI responses.**

A qualitative study could interview Gen-AI users and ask:

> "Tell me about a time when giving context significantly changed an AI response."

Suppose users repeatedly say:

* "I explained who the audience was."
* "I gave the AI my previous code."
* "I explained the purpose of the task."
* "Without the background information, the response was too generic."

The researcher can analyse these responses and identify themes such as:

```text
Context
 ├── User's goal
 ├── Background information
 ├── Existing work/code
 ├── Target audience
 └── Desired output
```

The goal is to **understand how users experience and perceive the role of context**, not simply calculate an average score.

---

# Qualitative vs Quantitative

| Qualitative                     | Quantitative                         |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Meaning and experience          | Measurement                          |
| Words / observations            | Numbers                              |
| Often open-ended                | Often structured                     |
| Themes and interpretation       | Statistical analysis                 |
| "Why / how?"                    | "How much / how many?"               |
| Deeper contextual understanding | Numerical measurement and comparison |

---

## Strengths

Qualitative research is useful when we want to:

* Understand complex human experiences.
* Explore a poorly understood phenomenon.
* Discover unexpected factors.
* Understand **why/how** something happens.
* Study phenomena in their real context.

## Limitation

Qualitative findings are often **context-rich but less directly suited to numerical generalisation** across large populations.

---

# Important: Qualitative Does NOT Mean "No Numbers"

A qualitative study may contain numbers.

For example:

> "8 of the 10 participants mentioned that providing the target audience improved their prompts."

That does not automatically make the study quantitative.

The classification depends mainly on the **research approach, type of data, and how the evidence is analysed**, not merely whether a number appears.

---

# Running Example

### Quantitative approach

> **"Does additional contextual information produce a measurable change in Gen-AI response quality?"**

→ Measure scores and analyse statistically.

### Qualitative approach

> **"How do users experience and understand the role of contextual information when interacting with Gen-AI?"**

→ Collect experiences and identify themes.
