# `High / Low`

### High / Low = `highest and lowest price the stock reached in a specific period`

Usually on stock websites (like Screener), it means **52-week High / Low**.

* **High** → highest price in the last **1 year**
* **Low** → lowest price in the last **1 year**

---

### Simple meaning

Think of it like a **price range of the last year** 📊

```text
High = Most expensive price people paid in last 1 year
Low  = Cheapest price people sold in last 1 year
```

---

### Example

Assume a stock during the last year traded like this:

| Month | Price |
| ----- | ----- |
| Jan   | ₹120  |
| Mar   | ₹150  |
| Jun   | ₹95   |
| Sep   | ₹180  |
| Dec   | ₹140  |

Result:

```text
52W High = ₹180
52W Low  = ₹95
```

---

### What it tells you

High / Low shows **how much the price has moved during the year**.

```text
Large gap  → highly volatile stock
Small gap  → stable stock
```

Example:

| Stock   | High | Low | Meaning       |
| ------- | ---- | --- | ------------- |
| Stock A | ₹100 | ₹95 | very stable   |
| Stock B | ₹100 | ₹40 | very volatile |

---

### How investors roughly use it

#### Near High

Price is close to the **top of its yearly range**

Possible interpretations:

* strong momentum 📈
* market very optimistic
* may be expensive

---

#### Near Low

Price is close to the **bottom of its yearly range**

Possible interpretations:

* market pessimistic
* temporary problem
* possible opportunity

---

### Important warning ⚠️

```text
Near Low ≠ Cheap
Near High ≠ Expensive
```

A stock may be near **Low because business is deteriorating**.

A stock may stay near **High because the company is very strong**.

---

### Very simple mental model

```text
Market Cap → company size
Current Price → last traded price
High / Low → yearly price range
```
