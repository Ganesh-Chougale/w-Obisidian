# 5.2 Selecting the Problem

## Core Idea

**Selecting a research problem = choosing a problem that is important, researchable, and feasible.**

A researcher may have many interesting ideas, but not every idea is suitable for research.

---

## Main Factors in Selecting a Research Problem

### 1. Researcher's Interest

The researcher should have genuine interest in the problem.

Why?

Research can take considerable time and effort, so sustained interest helps.

### 2. Researcher's Expertise

The problem should relate reasonably well to the researcher's knowledge, skills, or area of study.

### 3. Availability of Data

The required information/data must be obtainable.

> No access to necessary data → problem may not be practically researchable.

### 4. Importance of the Problem

The problem should have academic, scientific, social, or practical significance.

Ask:

> **"If I find the answer, does it matter?"**

### 5. Feasibility

The study should be possible within available:

* time
* money
* equipment/resources
* skills
* technology
* access to participants/data

### 6. Novelty / Research Gap

The problem should contribute something useful rather than unnecessarily repeating what is already well established.

Novelty does **not** always mean discovering something completely new. It may involve:

* a new population
* new context
* new method
* new dataset
* testing an existing finding in a different situation
* extending existing knowledge

### 7. Ethical Acceptability

The research should be capable of being conducted without violating ethical principles or unnecessarily harming participants.

---

## A Simple Selection Filter

Before selecting a problem, ask:

```text
Is it INTERESTING?
       ↓
Do I have / can I develop the EXPERTISE?
       ↓
Is DATA available?
       ↓
Is it IMPORTANT?
       ↓
Is it FEASIBLE?
       ↓
Does it offer useful NOVELTY / GAP?
       ↓
Can it be studied ETHICALLY?
       ↓
        YES
       ↓
SELECT THE PROBLEM
```

You don't necessarily need every factor to be perfect, but major obstacles should be identified before committing.

---

## Example

Suppose you want to study:

> **"Effect of contextual information on Gen-AI response quality."**

Check it:

* **Interest:** researcher is interested in Gen-AI.
* **Expertise:** understands Gen-AI and research methods sufficiently.
* **Data:** responses can be collected and evaluated.
* **Importance:** understanding context may improve Gen-AI use.
* **Feasibility:** experiment can potentially be conducted with available tools.
* **Novelty:** specific types/amounts/relevance of context may provide a research gap.
* **Ethics:** study can generally be designed without exposing sensitive information.

If these conditions are reasonably satisfied, the problem becomes a viable research candidate.

---

## One-second Memory

> **Good problem = Interesting + Important + Researchable + Feasible + Ethical + Useful contribution**

### PET Trap

**Interesting ≠ suitable research problem.**

An idea can be extremely interesting but still be unsuitable because:

* data cannot be obtained,
* it is too broad,
* it is not feasible,
* it has little significance,
* or it cannot be investigated ethically.
