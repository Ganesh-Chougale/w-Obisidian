## Goal: is to fetch valuable yield knowledge

## The source might be:
```
a 1-page article
a 300-page book
a 2-hour lecture
a 40-minute YouTube video
a research paper
a podcast
a conversation
any form of information
```

## Allowed
- stories
- historical background
- anecdotes

## Not Allowed
- marketing
- elaboration
- exaggeration
- filler
- yapping
- outright padding
basically "no sword fight with empty wind"


learning should not suppose to be `test of endurance`.
`Bad`:  
- 5 hours consuming → 30 minutes extracting.
- Finish the source.
- "How much of this source did I consume?"
- "I read 15 books this year."

`Good`:  
- 30 minutes consuming → 30 minutes understanding/extracting.
- Capture what the source has to offer.
- "What did this source actually give me?"
- "I extracted 40 ideas from 15 books and incorporated 8 of them into how I think and work."  


## Releif window
Doing all this but without going too far in the opposite direction.  
Because sometimes what appears to be "filler" is actually supporting structure.  
No aggressive forced compression.  
it might capture captured the sentence but lost the thinking.
So need to distinguish between  
1. Payload: The actual new knowledge.  
2. Support: Evidence, reasoning, examples and demonstrations that make the payload trustworthy or understandable.  
3. Packaging: Stories, repetition, transitions, anecdotes, motivational language, formatting, etc.  
4. Padding: Material whose removal would barely reduce understanding.  
goal isn't to eliminate support.  
goal is to eliminate unnecessary packaging + padding while preserving the payload + necessary support.  


## Philosophy
```
Content is a container. Knowledge is the payload.

The size of the container does not determine the amount of payload inside it.

The objective is not to fight every sentence, page, minute or paragraph. The objective is to identify what is valuable, understand why it is valuable, preserve the necessary reasoning behind it, and move forward.
```
not trying to disrespect long-form content.  
trying to avoid confusing consumption with learning.
