# 2.2 Applied vs Fundamental Research

## Core Difference

The simplest distinction:

> **Fundamental research → Builds knowledge.**
> **Applied research → Uses knowledge to solve a practical problem.**

---

## 1. Fundamental Research

### Meaning

**Fundamental research** (also called **basic or pure research**) aims to expand knowledge and understanding of a phenomenon without necessarily having an immediate practical application.

It mainly asks:

> **"What is happening, and how/why does it work?"**

### Our Example

Research topic:

> **The effect of contextual information on the quality of GEN-AI responses.**

A fundamental study might investigate:

> **How does contextual information influence the behaviour and response generation of Gen-AI systems?**

The goal is to understand the underlying relationship.

The researcher may discover principles about how context affects AI responses, even if there is no immediate product or practical solution.

### Key Point

**Immediate practical usefulness is not the primary objective.**

---

# 2. Applied Research

### Meaning

**Applied research** aims to use knowledge to address a **specific practical problem** or improve a real-world situation.

It mainly asks:

> **"How can we use this knowledge?"**

### Our Example

Suppose research discovers that certain types of context consistently improve response quality.

An applied study could use that finding to develop:

> **A practical framework for users on how to provide context to Gen-AI systems to obtain better responses.**

For example:

```text
Research finding
      ↓
Identify useful types of context
      ↓
Create practical guidelines
      ↓
Help users obtain better AI responses
```

The emphasis is on **practical application**.

---

# Key Difference

| Fundamental Research              | Applied Research                    |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Expands knowledge                 | Applies knowledge                   |
| Seeks deeper understanding        | Seeks practical solutions           |
| May have no immediate application | Targets a practical problem         |
| "How/why does this work?"         | "How can we use this?"              |
| Basic/pure research               | Practical problem-oriented research |

---

## Important Relationship

Fundamental and applied research are **not opposites where one is useful and the other is useless**.

Fundamental research can provide the knowledge that later enables applied research.

```text
Fundamental Research
        ↓
New Knowledge
        ↓
Applied Research
        ↓
Practical Solution
```

Applied research can also reveal new questions that lead back to fundamental research.

So the relationship can be:

```text
Knowledge ↔ Application
```

---

## Running Example

### Fundamental

> **"How does contextual information affect the generation and quality of Gen-AI responses?"**

→ Understand the phenomenon.

### Applied

> **"What type of contextual information should users provide to improve Gen-AI responses?"**

→ Use knowledge to solve a practical problem.

---

## PET Memory

> **Fundamental research = knowledge for understanding.**

> **Applied research = knowledge for practical use.**

