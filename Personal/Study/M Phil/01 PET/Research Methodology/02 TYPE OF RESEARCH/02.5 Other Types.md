# 2.5 Other Types of Research

Research can also be classified according to its **purpose, setting, method, or time dimension**.

## 1. Exploratory Research

Used when a problem is **not clearly understood** and the researcher wants to gain initial insights.

> **Question:** "What is going on here?"

**Our example:**

> Exploring what kinds of contextual information users provide to Gen-AI and what they consider useful.

---

## 2. Experimental Research

The researcher **deliberately changes one or more variables** and observes the effect.

> **Question:** "What happens if I change X?"

**Our example:**

Give the same Gen-AI task different levels/types of context and measure the resulting response quality.

```text
Change Context
      ↓
Observe Response
      ↓
Measure Quality
```

---

## 3. Historical Research

Studies **past events, records, documents, and evidence** to understand what happened and why.

**Our example:**

Studying the historical development of contextual prompting and how approaches to providing context to AI systems have evolved.

---

## 4. Diagnostic Research

Attempts to determine the **causes or underlying reasons for a problem**.

> **Question:** "Why is this problem happening?"

**Our example:**

Investigating why Gen-AI sometimes produces poor responses even when users provide substantial contextual information.

---

## 5. Action Research

Research conducted to **solve an immediate practical problem while simultaneously learning from the process**.

A researcher may:

```text
Identify Problem
      ↓
Take Action
      ↓
Observe Result
      ↓
Reflect
      ↓
Improve Action
      ↺
```

**Our example:**

A development team experiments with different context formats in its AI workflow, observes response quality, changes the approach, and repeats the cycle.

---

## 6. Field Research

Research conducted in the **real-world environment** where the phenomenon naturally occurs.

**Our example:**

Studying how people actually provide context to Gen-AI during their normal work rather than testing only in a controlled laboratory setting.

---

## 7. Laboratory Research

Research conducted in a **controlled environment**, where relevant conditions can be controlled more carefully.

**Our example:**

Running the same AI tasks under controlled conditions while systematically changing only the contextual information.

---

# High-Yield Distinctions

| Type             | Core idea                                      |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Exploratory**  | Explore an unclear problem                     |
| **Experimental** | Manipulate variables and observe effects       |
| **Historical**   | Study the past                                 |
| **Diagnostic**   | Find causes of a problem                       |
| **Action**       | Solve/improve an immediate practical situation |
| **Field**        | Study phenomenon in its natural setting        |
| **Laboratory**   | Study phenomenon under controlled conditions   |

## PET Memory

```text
Exploratory   → What is going on?
Experimental  → What happens if X changes?
Historical    → What happened in the past?
Diagnostic    → Why is the problem happening?
Action        → How can we improve the situation?
Field         → Study in the real world
Laboratory    → Study under controlled conditions
```

### Important

These classifications **can overlap**.

A single study can be, for example:

> **Applied + Quantitative + Empirical + Experimental + Laboratory research**

The categories describe **different aspects of a research study**, so they are not necessarily mutually exclusive.
