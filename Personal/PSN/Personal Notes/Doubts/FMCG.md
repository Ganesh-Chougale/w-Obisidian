FMCG stands for Fast-Moving Consumer Goods. These are products that sell quickly, are consumed frequently, and are usually priced relatively low. [1, 2, 3] 

The "Grocery Store" Analogy  
Think about a typical trip to the supermarket. Most of what you put in your cart—milk, bread, soap, or soda—falls under FMCG.  

* "Fast-Moving": They don't sit on shelves for years; they are bought and replaced weekly or even daily.  
* "Consumer Goods": They are items made for your immediate, everyday use rather than for a business to use in a factory.  

Core Characteristics  

* High Volume, Low Margin: Companies don't make much money on one chocolate bar, so they focus on selling millions of them.  
* Short Shelf Life: Many items are perishable (like dairy) or are used up quickly (like toothpaste).  
* Impulse Buys: Because they are cheap, you often buy them without much research or "deep thinking".    

Common Examples & Categories  

* Food & Drinks: Soft drinks, packaged snacks, milk, and bread.  
* Personal Care: Shampoo, toothpaste, soap, and cosmetics.  
* Household Items: Laundry detergent, dish soap, and toilet paper.  
* OTC Healthcare: Vitamins, aspirin, and basic bandages.   

Major Global Players  
The industry is dominated by "household name" giants that manage massive distribution networks:   

* Nestle: Known for coffee (Nescafé) and snacks (KitKat).
* Procter & Gamble (P&G): Famous for Tide, Gillette, and Pampers.
* Unilever: Makers of Dove, Lifebuoy, and Ben & Jerry's.
* Coca-Cola and PepsiCo: Dominating the global beverage and snack markets.
