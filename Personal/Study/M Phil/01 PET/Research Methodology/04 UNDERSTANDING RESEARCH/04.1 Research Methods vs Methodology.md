# 4.1 Research Methods vs Methodology

## Core Difference

> **Research Methods = the tools/techniques used to conduct research.**

> **Research Methodology = the reasoning and systematic framework behind choosing and using those methods.**

### One-second memory

```text
METHODS       → WHAT / HOW you collect & analyse data
METHODOLOGY   → WHY this approach & these methods are appropriate
```

---

# 1. Research Methods

### Meaning

**Research methods are the specific techniques and procedures used to collect, analyse, and interpret data.**

Examples:

* Questionnaire
* Interview
* Observation
* Experiment
* Sampling
* Statistical analysis
* Case study

### Our Example

Research topic:

> **The effect of contextual information on the quality of GEN-AI responses.**

Possible methods:

```text
Experiment
   ↓
Give AI the same task with different context
   ↓
Collect responses
   ↓
Score response quality
   ↓
Statistical analysis
```

Here, the **experiment, scoring procedure, and statistical analysis are methods**.

---

# 2. Research Methodology

### Meaning

**Research methodology is the overall logic, reasoning, and systematic approach used to conduct research and justify the methods selected.**

It addresses questions such as:

* Why is this research approach appropriate?
* Why use quantitative rather than qualitative methods?
* Why use an experiment?
* How should variables be defined and measured?
* How will the evidence answer the research question?
* Why is the chosen method suitable for the problem?

So methodology is **more than a list of methods**.

---

# Our Example

Suppose our research question is:

> **Does relevant contextual information affect Gen-AI response quality?**

We decide to use a **quantitative experimental approach**.

### Methods

We might:

1. Give the same task to the AI.
2. Vary the contextual information.
3. Collect the responses.
4. Score response quality.
5. Apply statistical analysis.

### Methodology

We need to justify **why** this design makes sense:

> Because the research asks whether changing contextual information produces a measurable difference in response quality, a quantitative experimental approach allows the contextual information to be systematically varied and the resulting response quality to be measured and compared.

That reasoning is **methodology**.

---

# Easy Analogy

Think about travelling.

### Method

> **Car, train, or airplane.**

These are the means used to travel.

### Methodology

> **Why did I choose the train for this journey, and why is that choice appropriate given the distance, time, cost, and purpose?**

So:

```text
METHOD
   ↓
The tool/technique

METHODOLOGY
   ↓
The reasoning behind the research approach and methods
```

---

# Key Difference

| Research Methods                                 | Research Methodology                                |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Specific techniques/procedures                   | Overall research logic and framework                |
| What/how research is carried out                 | Why/how the chosen approach is appropriate          |
| Questionnaire, interview, experiment, statistics | Justification and reasoning for the research design |
| Practical tools                                  | Systematic reasoning about the tools and approach   |

---

## Critical Point

> **Methodology is NOT simply another word for methods.**

A research paper saying:

> "We used questionnaires and statistical analysis."

is describing **methods**.

Explaining:

> "We chose a questionnaire because the research requires structured numerical data from a defined population, and statistical analysis is appropriate for examining the relationship between the variables."

is explaining **methodology**.
