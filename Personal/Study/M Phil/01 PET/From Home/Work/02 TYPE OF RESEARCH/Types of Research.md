## 2.1 Descriptive vs Analytical
`Descriptive`: tells what exists/happens.  
- example: the graph shows the user spending more time on app than used to do.  
`Analytical`: explains or examines why/how it exists/happens.  
- example: the findins shows that people are strugling within setting so it feels like they are spending more time on app.  
`Descriptive` => `Analytical`  

## 2.2 Fundamental vs Applied
`Fundamental`: develops general knowledge, theoretical frameworks, and basic scientific understanding without immediate practical goals.  
- example: Studying human psychology to map the exact cognitive limits of user attention spans.  
`Applied`: solves specific, practical, real-world problems facing individuals or organizations.  
- example: Redesigning your specific app's notification system to instantly lower user drop-off rates on that graph.  
`Fundamental` => `Applied`  

## 2.3 Quantitative vs Qualitative
`Quantitative`: measures numerical data, metrics, and statistical patterns to answer how much or how many.  
- example: new flagship phone has over 10k positive reviews. & most of them are 4/5 stars  
`Qualitative`: explores non-numerical insights, subjective meanings, and user experiences to answer why or how.  
- example: reading those actual reviews & knowing user liked the camera, screen, performance etc.  
`Quantitative` => `Qualitative`  

## 2.4 Conceptual vs Empirical
`Conceptual`: ideas, theories, principles, explaining “how/why might it happen?  
- example: Designing a theoretical smartphone layout on paper based on the principle that the thumb should easily reach all buttons.  
`Empirical`: observation real evidence, data & checks “does it actually happen?”
- example: Testing the physical prototype with 100 users to measure the exact drop-rates and thumb strain during daily use.  
`Conceptual` => `Empirical`
